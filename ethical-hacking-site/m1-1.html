Elements of Information Security

Before any attack or defense makes sense, you need the vocabulary. This page covers the properties every control protects — the CIA triad, the AAA model, non-repudiation — plus the challenges and the "defense in depth" mindset.

CIA triad AAA Non-repudiation Defense in depth
01

The CIA Triad

Core goals

C Confidentiality

Only authorized parties can see the data. Enforced by encryption, access control, least-privilege. Broken by disclosure, sniffing, shared creds.

I Integrity

Data is accurate and unaltered by unauthorized parties. Enforced by hashing, checksums, signatures. Broken by tampering, MITM modification.

A Availability

Authorized users can access data/systems when needed. Enforced by redundancy, patching, DDoS protection. Broken by DoS, ransomware, hardware failure.

PropertyQuestion it answersTypical control
ConfidentialityWho may read this?Encryption, ACLs, VPN
IntegrityHas it been changed?Hashing, digital signatures
AvailabilityIs it reachable now?Redundancy, DDoS mitigation

02

AAA & Other Goals

Access & accountability

A Authentication

Proving who you are — password, token, biometric, MFA.

A Authorization

What you are allowed to do once identified — permissions, roles, least privilege.

A Accounting

Logging/auditing what you did — the basis for event logs (see Module 5).

N Non-repudiation

A party cannot deny an action — achieved with digital signatures and strong audit trails.

Other stated goals: authenticity (the subject is genuine), possession (control of a token/asset), utility (data is useful in its intended form), and privacy (personal data handled per expectation/law).
03

Security Challenges & Attacks

The reality

Challenges

  • Complexity — large attack surface, interdependencies.
  • People — the weakest link; social engineering.
  • Legacy systems — unpatched, unsupported.
  • Usability vs security — tighter security often hurts UX.

Common attack types

  • Phishing / social engineering
  • Malware (virus, worm, ransomware)
  • Denial of Service (DoS/DDoS)
  • Man-in-the-Middle (MITM)
  • Insider threat
  • Credential theft

Defense in depth: layer multiple controls (perimeter + host + app + human) so no single failure compromises the whole system.
04

Threat Modeling & Risk

Thinking like a defender

Security work is about managing risk, not eliminating it. Risk = Likelihood × Impact: how probable an attack is, times how bad it would be. You spend effort where the product is highest.

Spoofing

Impersonating a user/system (fake identity).

Tampering

Altering data/code (breaks Integrity).

Repudiation

Denying an action was taken.

Info disclosure

Leaking data (breaks Confidentiality).

Denial of service

Breaking Availability.

Elevation

Gaining higher privileges than allowed.

Defense in depth in practice: pair controls so one failure doesn't sink the ship — firewall + IDS + patching + training.
05

Effects of Hacking

Why it matters

Individuals

  • Identity theft, drained bank accounts, ruined credit
  • Loss of private photos/messages, blackmail
  • Device hijacked into a botnet (slow, used for attacks)

Organizations

  • Financial loss: ransom, fraud, recovery cost
  • Breach of customer data → fines (GDPR/IT Act), lawsuits
  • Reputation damage, loss of trust, downtime
  • Theft of trade secrets, regulatory penalties
Why ethical hacking exists: the same skills used to break in are used, with permission, to find and fix the gaps before criminals do.
