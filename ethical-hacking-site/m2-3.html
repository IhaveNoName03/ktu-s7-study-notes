Social Engineering

The "human hacking" layer: manipulating people rather than breaking systems. The techniques below are why the strongest firewall still fails to a well-told lie.

Manipulation Phishing Human firewall
01

What & Why

Definition

Social engineering = tricking people into breaking security procedures or giving up sensitive info. It works because humans are trusting, helpful, and rushed. The insider threat — a trusted person abusing access — is the most dangerous variant.

The best defense is a "human firewall": trained, skeptical users who verify requests and report suspicious contact.

03

Defenses

Human firewall

Training & awareness

Teach users to recognize lures and verify requests out-of-band.

Verification policies

Confirm high-risk requests (wire transfers, password resets) by phone.

Clear procedures

Document how legit requests look; ban sharing creds.

Multi-factor auth

MFA blunts stolen-password attacks (avoid SMS-only — SIM swap).

04

Why It Works — Psychology

The lever

Authority

"I'm from IT, reset your password now" — people obey perceived superiors.

Urgency

"Your account closes in 10 minutes" kills careful thinking.

Familiarity / trust

Known brand or colleague name lowers guard.

Scarcity / reward

"Free gift / limited offer" triggers impulse.

05

Anatomy of a Phish & the Human Firewall

Dissection

A phishing email has four parts: sender (spoofed/lookalike domain), lure (urgent ask), payload (malicious link/attachment), and goal (creds/C2). Spotting any weak link breaks the chain.

Worked example — a credential phish

  1. Sender: security@micros0ft-support.com — a lookalike domain (zero vs o) impersonating Microsoft.
  2. Lure: "Unusual sign-in detected — secure your account in 10 minutes or it is locked." (authority + urgency).
  3. Payload: a button linking to a cloned login page that sends typed creds to the attacker.
  4. Goal: harvest the password, then reuse it (or the MFA prompt) to enter the real account.

Why the human firewall stops it: the user checks the real sender domain, pauses at the urgency, and verifies via a known bookmark / phone — never the link. MFA (app/TOTP, not SMS) then blunts even a stolen password.

Insider-threat case

A trusted contractor with legit DB access exfiltrates customer records to a personal cloud account over weeks. Controls that catch it: least-privilege access, DLP/ egress monitoring, and off-box logging so the activity is visible even though the person was "authorized."

Building the human firewall: simulated phishing drills, a "verify out-of-band" rule for money/access requests, and a no-blame reporting channel so users flag tries instead of hiding them.
