Construction Safety

Construction safety covers the project construction phase — different from operation/maintenance because civil, mechanical, electrical and storage activities happen simultaneously, raising the chance of accidents. This page covers the stages of a project, hazards by stage, scaffolds & ladders, excavation shoring, and ergonomics. Page 2 covers under-water works, tunneling, blasting, demolition, temporary structures, and the relevant Indian Standards & National Building Code.

01

Stages of Project Construction

CO3

Safety is important at every stage. The sequence:

  1. Civil works
  2. Receiving & storage of materials / equipment / spares
  3. Erection
  4. Testing & commissioning
  5. First energizing
  6. Trial operations
  7. Handing over to operating staff

Because several activities run at once, the probability of accidents is higher than in steady operation.

Common construction injuries: falls from height, being struck by falling objects, trench/excavation collapse, crane/sling failure, and fire/explosion during hot work.
03

Scaffolds, Temporary Structures & Ladders

Access safety

A scaffold is a temporary wooden or metal framework/platform that supports workmen or material during construction, erection, repair or painting. Prefer steel scaffolds; they must be competently erected, inspected, and load-rated.

Scaffold safety: stable base and foundations, proper bracing, guard-rails and toe-boards on working platforms, secure access, and regular inspection by a competent person. Never overload beyond its rated capacity.

Scaffold parts (know the names): standards (vertical members), ledgers (horizontal members along the face), putlogs/transoms (horizontal members across the width supporting the plank), planks/boards (the working platform), guard rails and toe boards (edge protection), and couplers (the fittings that join the tubes). A competent person inspects and tags scaffolds (green = safe, red = do not use) before use and after any event that could affect stability.

Ladders: of good construction, sound material and adequate strength. Do not use a ladder with a defective or missing rung, or one whose rung depends solely on nails/spikes. Secure the ladder (tie top and bottom), maintain a safe pitch (~75° / 1:4), and do not over-reach. Use three-point contact (two hands and one foot, or two feet and one hand) when climbing, and extend the top at least ~1 m above the landing.

Excavation areas must be adequately lit for night work, and public sidewalks protected with barriers and warning signs. Prevent trench cave-in by sloping/benching the sides, or by shoring (timber/steel supports) or underpinning when close to existing structures — never stand in an unsupported trench.
04

Ergonomics in Construction

Strain injuries

Ergonomics fits the job/task to the worker to reduce injury risk. An ergonomic hazard is a condition that can lead to a Musculoskeletal Disorder (MSD).

Cumulative Trauma Disorders (CTDs) / repetitive-strain injuries are soft-tissue injuries from repeated exposure to an ergonomic stressor. Examples: carpal tunnel syndrome, tendonitis, thoracic outlet syndrome, tension-neck syndrome. Because almost all work uses the arms and hands, most construction WMSDs affect the hands, wrists, elbows and shoulders.

Major ergonomic risks in construction: heavy / awkward manual lifting, repetitive hand tool use, prolonged awkward postures, vibration from power tools, and kneeling/working at low levels.

Control: use mechanical aids (hoists, dollies), train lifting technique, rotate tasks, provide antivibration gloves, and design workstations/tool handles to keep wrists neutral. Safe manual lifting: plan the lift and clear the path; stand close to the load with feet apart; bend the knees (not the back); grip firmly; lift smoothly using the legs; keep the load close to the body; avoid twisting — turn by moving the feet; and get help or use aids for heavy or awkward loads.
