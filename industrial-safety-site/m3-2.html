Construction Safety — Other Operations & Codes

Page 2 of Module 3. We cover the construction operations named in the syllabus that need their own safe systems: under-water works, tunneling, blasting, demolition, temporary structures, and — a specific exam item — relevant Indian Standards and the National Building Code (NBC) provisions on construction safety. (Confined space appears in Module 2; it is summarised there.)

Under-water Tunneling Blasting IS / NBC
05

Under-water Works

Special operation

Under-water / marine construction covers foundation works, piling, caisson sinking, cofferdams and repairs carried out partly or fully below water (rivers, harbours, offshore). It combines normal construction hazards with drowning, entrapment in silt/debris, decompression issues for divers, and limited visibility/communication.

Key controls: only trained, certified divers; a standby diver and a bank-side supervisor with communication; a permit-to-work with isolation of nearby plant; current/weather and depth limits; decompression procedures per dive tables; rescue and resuscitation equipment on site; and protection of the water against pollution from the works.

Why it is high-risk: escape and rescue are slow, the environment is hostile, and a small equipment failure becomes life-threatening — so under-water work needs the tightest permit, training and standby arrangements of any construction operation.
06

Tunneling

Confined & deep

Tunneling drives openings through soil/rock for transport, utilities or mining. Hazards are a mix of ground collapse, inrush of water/gas, poor air (oxygen deficiency, diesel exhaust, blasting fumes), fire/explosion (especially in gassy ground), and plant/transport strikes inside a narrow space.

Key controls: proper ground support (rings, shotcrete, fore-poling) as the face advances; continuous atmospheric monitoring for oxygen, CO, CO₂, methane and dust; forced ventilation with clean-air ducts to the face; strict firing/blasting rules and clearance; mechanical handling with walkways and trip devices; and a tested emergency evacuation and refuge plan.

Link to Module 2: tunneling is a confined-space operation — the same entry-permit, testing, ventilation, isolation and stand-by principles from the confined-space section apply, intensified because the space is long, deep and moving.
07

Blasting

Explosives

Blasting in construction (rock breaking, trenching, demolition) uses explosives that must be stored, transported, charged and fired under licence and strict procedure. The book also covers sand/abrasive blasting (Ch.3.26) — projecting abrasive by compressed air/steam for surface cleaning — which has its own hazards (silica dust, rebound, noise).

Controlled-explosive blasting controls: a blast plan and permit; licensed, trained blast crew; secure magazines and isolation during transit; exclusion zone and warning signals; effective blast timing and misfire procedure; and clearance checks before anyone re-enters.

Abrasive (sand) blasting controls: use of less-toxic abrasive where possible, local exhaust ventilation, abrasive-blast cabinets or rooms, RPE for the operator, and hearing protection for the noise.

Silica is a serious health hazard: sand blasting generates respirable crystalline silica that causes silicosis — so substitute (e.g. garnet, steel grit) or fully enclose and ventilate; this is a long-term disease control, not just an acute-injury control.
08

Demolition & Temporary Structures

Controlled teardown

Demolition is higher-risk than building because loads and stability are unknown and the structure is being weakened on purpose. Follow a demolition plan: survey the structure and services (gas, water, electricity, asbestos), isolate and disconnect them, protect the public with hoarding/barriers, work top-down with mechanical methods where possible, and keep plant and people clear of the collapse zone.

Temporary structures — scaffolds, temporary roofs, access towers, shoring and formwork — must be designed, erected by competent people, inspected (tagged) and maintained; they fail by overload, poor base or wind if treated casually. Refer to the scaffold parts and shoring notes on page 1.

Common demolition injuries: falling from height, struck by falling debris, collapse of an unexpected element, exposure to hazardous materials released, and manual-handling injury — so the plan must address each before work starts.
09

Indian Standards & the National Building Code

Syllabus reference

The syllabus asks for familiarization with relevant Indian Standards (IS) and the National Building Code (NBC) provisions on construction safety. These are the codified rules that the general principles above are drawn from. Key references for construction safety:

CodeCoverage (construction safety)
IS 3696 (Parts 1 & 2)Safety code for scaffolds and ladders — materials, construction, safe working load, inspection.
IS 3766Specification for safety requirements in the erection of concrete structures (formwork, falsework, loads).
IS 4014 (Parts 1 & 2)Code of practice for laying and safe use of ladders — wood and aluminium alloy.
IS 11375 / IS 11592Safety code for conveyors and material-handling plant used on site.
National Building Code (NBC) Part 9Fire & life safety, and the construction-site safety / temporary works provisions referenced across the code.
Factories Act 1948 (relevant sections)General duties and site-safety obligations the IS codes implement.
How to use this in the exam: you are not expected to memorise every clause — know that scaffolds follow IS 3696, ladders IS 4014, formwork/falsework IS 3766, and that the NBC is the umbrella construction code. Cite the relevant IS when explaining a control.
