01

Hazard, Risk & Types of Hazards

Basics

Hazard: a source with potential to cause harm. Risk: the likelihood × severity of that harm. You identify hazards, then assess/control risk.

Common hazard types

  • Mechanical, electrical, thermal
  • Chemical, biological
  • Ergonomic, psychological
  • Fire/explosion, radiation, noise

Fire / explosion / toxic release

Three major process-safety consequences: unwanted fire, explosion (rapid energy release), and toxic-gas release (exposure/cloud). Each needs specific prevention & mitigation.

ClassFuelExtinguisher / method
AOrdinary combustibles (wood, paper, cloth)Water, foam, ABC powder
BFlammable liquids (oil, petrol, solvents)Foam, CO₂, ABC/BC powder (never water jet)

Remember: match extinguisher to class. Water on B/D/electrical can spread fire or shock. Selection depends on the fire's fuel type.
The fire triangle (and tetrahedron): a fire needs three things — fuel, heat (ignition/energy) and oxygen. Remove any one and the fire goes out. Adding the chemical chain reaction gives the fire tetrahedron. Extinguishers act by taking away one side: water/foam cool (remove heat) and blanket (exclude oxygen); dry powder smothers by cutting oxygen and inhibits the chain reaction; CO₂ displaces oxygen (clean agent, good for electronics, not for class A); wet-chemical cools and forms a foam blanket on burning oils (class F).
Fig · Fire Classes & First Response

Six classes by fuel. Pick the extinguisher for the fuel type — never water on metal, liquid, or live-electrical fires.

03

Structure of HIRA

Process

Hazard Identification & Risk Assessment steps:

  1. Identify hazards (inventory, walkthrough, what-if)
  2. Analyze consequences & likelihood
  3. Estimate risk (rank)
  4. Evaluate against tolerability criteria
  5. Control (eliminate → substitute → engineer → administrate → PPE)
  6. Monitor & review
04

Dow Fire & Explosion Hazard Index

Quantitative

The Dow Fire & Explosion Hazard Index (F&EI) is a screening tool rating the relative fire/explosion risk of a process plant. It combines:

  • Material factor (energy of the material)
  • Process hazard factors (temperature, pressure, oxidation, etc.)
  • Special hazard factors (toxicity, nearby units)

The resulting F&EI maps to a radius of exposure and a unit hazard classification (light → extreme), guiding layout, isolation, and mitigation. Used in the design/review of process plants.

05

Preliminary Hazard Analysis (PHA) & HAZOP

CO5

PHA

Early, broad study to identify hazards and rank them before detailed design — what could go wrong and its severity.

HAZOP (Hazard & Operability Study)

Structured team review of a design/process using guide-words applied to parameters to find deviations from intent.

HAZOP methodology

  • Break the process into nodes (sections).
  • For each node, take a parameter (flow, pressure, temperature, level…).
  • Apply a guide-word (No / More / Less / Reverse / Part-of / As-well-as / Other-than) to get a deviation (e.g. No flow, More pressure).
  • Ask causes → consequences → existing safeguards → recommendations.
  • Document actions and owners.
Example: node = reactor feed; parameter = flow; guide-word = More → deviation More flow → cause (valve stuck open) → consequence (overfill/reaction runaway) → safeguard (high-flow alarm) → action (add interlock).

For each node: pick a parameter, apply a guide-word → deviation; then work causes → consequences → safeguards → recommendations (with owners).

06

Criticality Analysis & Chemical Hazards

Control

Criticality analysis

Ranks items/failure modes by severity × likelihood × detectability to prioritise corrective action and follow-up. Drives where to act first.

Chemical hazards

Flammability, toxicity, reactivity, corrosivity. Control via substitution, containment, ventilation, PPE, and procedure — informed by the MSDS.

Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS / SDS)

  • Identity, hazards, composition
  • First-aid, firefighting, accidental-release measures
  • Handling/storage, exposure limits, PPE
  • Physical/chemical properties, stability/reactivity, toxicology
  • Disposal, transport, regulatory info
MSDS is the authoritative reference for safely handling any chemical — read it before the job, not after an incident.
