PCA & Dimensionality Reduction

When features are many and correlated, Principal Component Analysis (PCA) rotates the data to new axes — the directions of largest variance — and keeps only the top ones. It compresses data, removes redundancy, and visualizes high dimensions, with almost no loss of information.

Variance Eigen Projection
01

The Idea

Concept

Given d-dimensional data, PCA finds d orthogonal directions (principal components) ordered by how much variance they capture. Project onto the first k components to get a k-dimensional representation that keeps as much variance as possible.

Z = WT X

where the columns of W are the top-k eigenvectors of the data's covariance matrix, and Z is the reduced data.

Why reduce? fewer features → faster training, less overfitting, and you can plot 2-D/3-D to see structure. PCA is unsupervised — it only looks at variance, not labels.
02

Covariance & Eigen-decomposition

Math

Steps of PCA:

  1. Center the data (subtract the mean of each feature).
  2. Compute the covariance matrix Σ = (1/n) XT X.
  3. Eigen-decompose Σ = V Λ VT; eigenvectors V are the principal components.
  4. Sort eigenvectors by eigenvalue Λ (variance along that component), keep the top k.
  5. Project: Z = VkT X.
variance along component j = λj   (fraction kept = Σj≤k λj / Σall λj)
Choosing k: keep enough components to retain, say, 95% of total variance. A scree plot (eigenvalue vs index) shows the "elbow" where extra components add little.
03

Worked — 2-D to 1-D

Worked

Centered 2-D points roughly lie along a tilted line. The covariance matrix has eigenvectors v₁ (the long direction, large λ₁) and v₂ (the short direction, small λ₂). Projecting onto v₁ keeps most of the spread:

fraction kept = λ₁ / (λ₁ + λ₂)

If λ₁ = 9 and λ₂ = 1, one component already retains 90% of the variance — so dropping v₂ (the near-zero-variance axis) barely loses information. This is why PCA can compress 100 correlated features down to 10–20 without hurting model accuracy.

Rotation view: PCA is just a rotation of the axes so the new x-axis points where the data is widest. The short axis is mostly noise and can be dropped.
