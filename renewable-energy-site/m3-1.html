Wind Energy

The kinetic energy of moving air, converted to electricity by a Wind Energy Conversion System (WECS). This page covers the turbine parts, the wind-power equation, the Betz limit, capacity factor, rotors, and site selection.

01

Wind Energy — What & Why

Origin

Wind = kinetic energy of air in motion. It is a form of solar energy caused by three concurrent events:

  • The Sun unevenly heating the atmosphere
  • Irregularities of the Earth's surface
  • The rotation of the Earth
Air flows because the Earth's surface is heated unevenly by radiant solar energy. Capture it with a Wind Energy Conversion System (WECS).
02

Major Parts of a Horizontal-Axis Wind Turbine

Anatomy

TTower

Supports everything and lifts the rotor to safe height + stronger wind. Height depends on power capacity. Must withstand max observed wind speed.

NNacelle

Box on top of tower housing generator, power converter, gearbox, controller, cables, yaw drive.

BRotor blades

Convert wind → mechanical energy. Must be strong (storms) yet light (smooth rotation). Wind on blades → rotation → shaft.

SShaft

Low-speed shaft from hub; coupled to generator directly (low-speed gen) or via gearbox.

GGearbox

Speed multiplier. e.g. ratio 1:80, 15 rpm in → 1200 rpm out for the generator.

EGenerator

Mechanical → electrical. Modern turbines use induction generators.

PPitch drive

Motors rotate blade angle to capture max wind as speed changes ("pitching").

YYaw drive

Motor rotates the nacelle to face the wind direction for max energy capture.

+WECS types

Classified by rotor axis (HAWT/VAWT), and by how they react to wind (lift/drag).

Fig · Horizontal-axis Wind Turbine

Wind turns the rotor (pitch-controlled blades) → nacelle houses gearbox + generator → tower supports it; yaw keeps it facing the wind.

03

Wind Power Equation

Derivation

Consider air flowing through an imaginary duct. Mass m passes per second (mass flow rate). Velocity at inlet = V₁, at outlet = V₂; velocity at turbine = average Vₐ.

Kinetic energy at inlet = ½·m·V₁² ; at outlet = ½·m·V₂². Change in KE per second = power extracted.

m = ρ · Vₐ · A  →  P = ½ · ρ · A · Vₐ · (V₁² − V₂²)

With Vₐ = (V₁ + V₂)/2, substituting gives the standard form. To maximise P, differentiate w.r.t. V₂ and set to zero → this yields the Betz result below.

Worked example (from syllabus): blades 12 m diameter, wind 6 m/s, ρ = 1.2 kg/m³, Cₚ(max) = 0.35. Area A = π(6)² ≈ 113 m². Ideal power P = ½·ρ·A·V³ = ½·1.2·113·216 ≈ 14,635 W. With Cₚ=0.35 → usable ≈ 5,122 W (~5.1 kW).
04

The Betz Coefficient (Betz Limit)

Max efficiency

Differentiating the power equation shows the theoretical maximum fraction of wind kinetic power that can be extracted is 0.5925 (≈59.3%). This is the Betz Coefficient / Betz limit — no turbine can exceed it.

Power coefficient Cₚ = actual power / (½·ρ·A·V³). Real turbines reach Cₚ ≈ 0.35–0.45; the system efficiency overall is ~35–44%.

Betz proved it by setting dP/dV₂ = 0; the optimum is when V₂ = ⅓·V₁ (downstream velocity one-third of upstream).
05

Capacity Factor

How well-sited?

Capacity factor = actual output ÷ maximum possible output, over a year (%). It shows how available the source is throughout the year and how well-sited the turbine is. Closer to 100% = better availability.

A wind plant may have high nameplate capacity but a modest capacity factor because wind is intermittent.

Blade Aerodynamics — Aerofoil, Angle of Attack, Tip-Speed Ratio

Why lift-type wins

A wind turbine blade is an aerofoil (airfoil) — the same shape as an aircraft wing. As wind flows over the curved upper surface and flatter lower surface, air moves faster on top, lowering pressure there. The pressure difference produces lift perpendicular to the wind, and a smaller drag parallel to it. Lift-type rotors (aerofoil blades) are far more efficient than drag-type (e.g. Savonius) because most of the useful force is lift.

The angle of attack is the angle between the blade chord line and the oncoming wind. A small angle gives steady lift; as the angle grows, lift rises until it suddenly collapses at the stall angle (~15°) — the blade "stalls" and loses lift. Pitch control changes the blade angle to keep the angle of attack near the efficient range and to shed power in storms.

The tip-speed ratio (TSR, λ) = rotor tip speed ÷ wind speed = (ω·R)/V. It tells how fast the blade tips move relative to the wind. Low TSR → drag-dominated (savonius); high TSR (λ ≈ 6–8 for 3-blade HAWT) → lift-dominated and efficient. There is an optimal TSR that maximises the power coefficient Cₚ; operating off it reduces Cₚ and thus output.

Why 3 blades? One/two blades raise TSR but worsen balance and noise; many blades lower TSR toward drag-type. Three blades is the practical optimum for efficiency, balance, and acceptable sound.
07

Site Selection for Wind Plants

Where to build
  • High average wind velocity: 6–30 m/s year-round.
  • Away from cities/forests (buildings & trees resist wind).
  • Measure wind at several heights — velocity rises with height.
  • Tower must survive the max wind speed observed in recent years.
08

Advantages & Disadvantages

Balanced

Advantages

  • Free, inexhaustible source
  • Clean, non-polluting
  • Low maintenance cost
  • Low generation cost (~₹2.25/kWh)

Disadvantages

  • High capital cost (~₹3.5 crore/MW)
  • Very fluctuating → needs storage for continuous supply
  • Storms can damage windmills
  • System efficiency only 35–44%
  • Sound pollution (audible km away)
