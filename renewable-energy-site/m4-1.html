Biomass Energy

Energy from organic matter — forests, farms, urban refuse, agro-waste. This page covers biomass resources, the six biofuels, conversion technologies (gasification, ethanol, biogas), the KVIC and Janata plant designs, factors affecting biogas, and India's biomass program.

01

Biomass Resources & Biofuels

Overview

Biomass = organic, carbon-based matter from plants/animals. Dry biomass gives heat by direct combustion; conversion tech turns it into high-quality solid/liquid/gaseous biofuels. Resources come from forests, rural farms, urban refuse, and agro-industry waste.

Disadvantages of biomass: dispersed & land-intensive; low energy density; labour-intensive collection; capacity tied to availability (not good for varying loads); not feasible everywhere.

02

Biomass Gasification

Thermo-chemical

Thermo-chemical conversion of solid biomass into a combustible gas through partial combustion (restricted oxygen) with no solid carbon residue. Output gas = wood gas / producer gas / syngas — mainly CO + H₂ (+ small CH₄), plus SOₓ/NOₓ depending on feedstock.

Gasifier types (fixed-bed):

Updraft

Fuel and air move counter-current (opposite directions).

Downdraft

Fuel and air move co-current. In a typical downdraft gasifier: drying (200 °C) → pyrolysis (200–600 °C, char + acetic acid + methanol + water vapour) → … → combustible gas at bottom.

Basic reaction zones are the same in both; only flow direction differs.
03

Ethanol from Biomass

Fermentation/Chemical

Ethyl alcohol (C₂H₅OH) — renewable petroleum substitute. Feedstock by category:

  • Sugars: sugarcane, sugar beet, sweet sorghum, grapes, molasses
  • Starches: maize, wheat, barley, potato, cassava, rice
  • Cellulose: wood, straw, grass stems, bamboo, bagasse

Sugar/starch feedstocks ferment directly; cellulose needs pre-treatment. Ethanol can substitute petrol (blended or pure).

04

Biogas Production

Anaerobic digestion

A biogas plant converts cattle dung + organic matter into inflammable biogas + quality organic manure under anaerobic conditions. Two popular designs:

1. Floating-drum (constant pressure)

KVIC model (standardised 1961): underground cylindrical masonry digester with inlet (dung slurry) + outlet (sludge) pipes; a steel dome floats on the slurry, moving up/down with gas accumulation, guided by a shaft. A partition wall improves circulation for fermentation.

2. Fixed-dome (constant volume)

Janata model: fixed dome; gas collected in a dome, slurry level varies. Lower cost, less moving parts.

Factors affecting biogas generation

  • Feedstock C:N ratio and organic content
  • Temperature (mesophilic ~30–35 °C optimum)
  • pH (near neutral)
  • Retention time / loading rate
  • Mixing and absence of oxygen (strictly anaerobic)
05

Urban Waste → Energy & India Program

Context

Urban waste to energy

Municipal/agro waste is a major biomass resource; current commercial use leans on residues collected for other reasons (timber/food processing, urban refuse).

Future in India (4 segments)

  • Process heat in biomass-waste industries
  • Cooking energy (charcoal, briquettes)
  • Electricity generation
  • Transport liquid fuels
Biomass program's future lies in modern conversion technologies (gasification, enzymatic ethanol, efficient digesters).
