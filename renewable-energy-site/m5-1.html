Small Hydro, Fuel Cells & Hydrogen Energy

The final module ties together small-scale hydro and the two emerging electrochemical technologies — fuel cells (with their classifications) and hydrogen energy (production, storage, use). Includes the exam diagrams: mini-hydro layout, phosphoric-acid and PEM fuel cells.

Small Hydro Power

Classification

Hydro projects classified by capacity:

ClassCapacity range
Micro hydroup to ~100 kW
Mini hydro~100 kW – 2 MW (sometimes up to 5 MW)
Small hydroup to ~25–30 MW (regional definition varies)

Basic concepts & turbines

  • Potential energy of stored/flowing water → mechanical (turbine) → electrical (generator).
  • Turbine types: impulse (Pelton) for high head/low flow; reaction (Francis, Kaplan) for medium/low head/high flow.
  • Selection considerations: head, flow rate, site geology, environmental flow, grid/load proximity.
Mini-hydro layout (exam diagram): intake/weir → desilting chamber → headrace channel → forebay → penstock → turbine → generator → transmission; tailrace returns water to river. Include survival/ecological flow.
Fig · Mini Hydro Project Layout

Water is diverted at the intake, settles in the forebay, drops through the penstock to spin the turbine, drives the generator, and returns via the tailrace (keep ecological flow).

Fuel Cells — Principle & Classification

Electrochemical

A fuel cell is an electrochemical device that converts the chemical energy of a fuel (H₂) and an oxidant (O₂) directly into electricity — like a battery that is continuously fed reactants. No combustion, so high efficiency and low emissions.

Principle: H₂ → anode (oxidation, electrons to external circuit); O₂ → cathode (reduction); ions move through an electrolyte; product is water + heat. Stacking cells raises voltage/current.

Classification (by electrolyte)

TypeElectrolyteOperating temp
PEM / PEMFC (Proton Exchange Membrane)Polymer membrane~50–100 °C
PAFC (Phosphoric Acid)Phosphoric acid~150–200 °C
SOFC (Solid Oxide)Ceramic oxide~600–1000 °C
MCFC (Molten Carbonate)Molten carbonate~600 °C
AFC (Alkaline)KOH solution~80 °C
DMFC (Direct Methanol)Polymer + methanol~50–100 °C

Conversion efficiency & losses: efficiency = useful electrical output ÷ fuel enthalpy. Losses come from activation polarisation, ohmic (IR) losses, concentration/mass-transport losses, and parasitic auxiliaries. Real stacks reach ~40–60% (higher with cogenerated heat).

Applications: stationary power, vehicles, portable/backup power, space programs.

Fig · PEM Fuel Cell (H₂ + O₂ → H₂O + electricity)

At the anode H₂ → 2H⁺ + 2e⁻; H⁺ crosses the polymer membrane to the cathode; e⁻ power the load; at the cathode O₂ + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂O (only product). PAFC is the same idea with a liquid phosphoric-acid electrolyte.

03

PAFC & PEM — Exam Diagrams

Worked

Phosphoric Acid Fuel Cell (PAFC)

H₂ + O₂ → electricity + water + heat. H₂ enters anode, splits into H⁺ + e⁻; H⁺ passes through liquid phosphoric-acid electrolyte to cathode; e⁻ travel the external circuit (power); O₂ + H⁺ + e⁻ → H₂O at cathode. Operates ~150–200 °C; mature, used in stationary CHP.

PEM Fuel Cell

Solid polymer membrane conducts H⁺. H₂ oxidised at anode → H⁺ + e⁻; membrane passes H⁺ to cathode; e⁻ power the load; at cathode O₂ + H⁺ + e⁻ → H₂O. Low-temp (~50–100 °C), fast start, good for vehicles/portables.

04

Hydrogen Energy

Production · Storage · Use

Production methods

  • Electrolysis: split water (H₂O) into H₂ + O₂ using electricity (clean if power is renewable). 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂.
  • Thermo-chemical: high-temperature processes — steam methane reforming (CH₄ + H₂O → CO + 3H₂), thermolysis, and other cycles (e.g. sulphur-iodine) that release H₂ from water/feedstock.
  • Also from biomass gasification and partial oxidation.

Storage & utilization

  • Storage: compressed gas, liquid H₂ (cryogenic), metal hydrides, chemical carriers.
  • Utilization: fuel cells (electricity), combustion/IC engines, industrial feedstock, and as a clean energy carrier.
Hydrogen is an energy carrier, not a primary source — its cleanliness depends on how it is made (green H₂ from renewables vs grey from fossil).
