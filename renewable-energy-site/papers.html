Module 1 — Sample Questions (Ktunotes.in)

10 questions
M13Write short notes on the advantages and disadvantages of any three types of non-conventional energy sources.
→ M1-1 (Non-conventional)

Solar: adv — non-polluting, renewable, low running cost; lim — intermittent, weather-dependent, low efficiency. Wind: adv — free, large potential (1.6×10⁷ MW); lim — noisy, costly mills, unreliable. Biomass/Biogas: adv — from waste, renewable; lim — biogas causes GHG, needs feedstock. (Any three with both sides.)

M15Differentiate between flat plate collectors and solar concentrators.
→ M1-2 (Collectors)

Flat-plate (non-concentrating): absorbs beam + diffuse, no tracking, low–medium temp, strong/low-maintenance, works on cloudy days, concentration ≈1 (≈4 with boosters). Concentrators (focus): beam only, need tracking, high temp (up to ~3000 °C), moving parts/higher maintenance, poor on hazy days, concentration 10–3000×.

M15Discuss advantages and limitations of conventional energy sources.
→ M1-1 (Conventional)

Advantages: cheaper now; easy storage → assured availability/security; universal conversion tech, convenient to use. Limitations: pollutants (CO, CO₂, particulates, heat) → health/environment; coal is valuable petrochem feedstock (conserve); non-renewable → will finish; large hydro disrupts wildlife/forests.

M110With the aid of a neat diagram, explain type solar thermal electric plant.
→ M1-2 (Solar Thermal)

Diagram: Solar collectors → heat-transfer fluid → boiler/steam drum → turbo-generator → grid; with a storage tank and pump in the loop. Working: collectors (flat-plate or concentrator) absorb solar radiation as heat and pass it to a fluid; the fluid makes steam in a boiler; steam drives a turbo-generator exactly like a conventional thermal plant; excess heat is stored. Concentrators can reach high temp for higher Rankine efficiency; flat-plate suits low-grade preheating. Label collector, pump, boiler, turbine, generator, condenser, grid.

M15Define (i) Open Circuit Voltage (ii) Short Circuit Current (iii) Fill factor (iv) Efficiency of the solar cell.
→ M1-3 (I–V)

(i) Voc: terminal voltage at zero current (open circuit). (ii) Isc: current at zero voltage (short circuit). (iii) Fill factor FF = (Vm·Im)/(Voc·Isc); quality of the I–V curve, typical 0.5–0.83. (iv) Efficiency η = (max output Vm·Im) / (solar energy incident on the cell).

M110Compare the components and working of a stand-alone and grid-connected PV system.
→ M1-3 (Systems)

Stand-alone: array + MPPT + battery storage + inverter at the load centre (remote village). Meets local load; battery stores excess for night; surplus may be dumped to heaters. No grid. Grid-connected (interactive): array meets local load first, exports surplus to grid in sunshine; two-way meter; no battery needed; grid supplies when sun is low. Difference: storage (battery vs none), grid tie (no vs yes), intermittency handling (battery vs grid), cost (higher vs lower).

M15How are energy resources classified? Compare conventional and non-conventional sources.
→ M1-1 (Classification)

Classification axes: (1) usability — primary/intermediate/secondary; (2) traditional use — conventional/non-conventional; (3) renewability — renewable/non-renewable; (4) commercial — commercial/non-commercial; (5) origin — fossil/nuclear/hydro/solar/wind/biomass/geothermal/ocean. Compare: conventional = used for decades, cheaper now, polluting, non-renewable; non-conventional = renewable, eco-friendly, low running cost, but high harnessing cost + weather-dependent + low efficiency.

M110Explain construction of solar flat plate collector with a neat diagram.
→ M1-2 (Flat-plate)

Diagram: transparent glass cover on top → blackened absorber plate (copper/aluminium/steel) → fluid tubes in contact with absorber → thermal insulation underneath → tight box. Parts: (i) absorber plate intercepts radiation (coating cuts re-emission); (ii) transparent cover lets radiation in, blocks reradiation/convection loss; (iii) fluid tubes carry heat away; (iv) insulation minimises loss; (v) box protects all. No tracking → strong, low maintenance; absorbs direct + diffuse.

M110Draw the block diagram of a solar thermal electric plant and explain its working.
→ M1-2 (Solar Thermal)

Block diagram: Solar collector field → heat-transfer fluid loop → boiler (makes steam) → turbo-generator → electric grid; storage tank + pump. Working: collectors absorb solar heat → fluid carries it to a boiler → steam expands through turbine → generator produces electricity → condenser returns water. Same Rankine cycle as a fossil plant, but the "boiler" is solar-heated. Surplus heat is stored for use when the sun is weak.

M110Discuss the effect of temperature and insolation on the characteristics of a solar cell.
→ M1-3 (I–V)

Insolation ↑: more photons → Isc rises (curve shifts up); Voc rises slightly → more power and a higher Pm. Temperature ↑: diode leakage grows → Voc falls → power and fill factor drop (cells work better when cool). Net: power tracks sunlight intensity and inversely tracks temperature; MPPT tracks the moving Pm.

M110Draw the P–V characteristics of a solar cell under varying temperature and irradiation level.
→ M1-3 (I–V)

The P–V curve is the product V×I, a hump peaking at Pm. Higher irradiation → taller, broader hump (Isc up) → higher Pm. Higher temperature → hump shrinks and shifts slightly left (Voc down) → lower Pm and FF. So at fixed temp, more sun = more power; at fixed sun, hotter cell = less power. Sketch several humps: largest at high-irr/low-temp, smallest at low-irr/high-temp.

Module 2 — Sample Questions (Ktunotes.in)

7 questions
M210With the help of a block diagram explain the working of a hybrid OTEC.
→ M2-1 (OTEC)

Diagram: warm surface seawater → vacuum flash chamber → vapour; that vapour evaporates a separate ammonia loop → turbine → generator; cold deep seawater condenses the ammonia (recycled) and also condenses the flashed seawater into freshwater. Working: hybrid = open + closed. Warm seawater is flash-evaporated in vacuum (open step); the vapour boils ammonia in a closed Rankine loop driving a turbine/generator; cold seawater condenses both streams. Bonus: the condensed flashed seawater is potable freshwater.

M25List out the advantages and disadvantages of a tidal power plant.
→ M2-2 (Tidal)

Advantages: pollution-free, inexhaustible; dam at bays (no village uprooting/eco disruption like large hydro); no land submergence; basins for fish farming; not weather-dependent (tide cycle definite). Disadvantages: needs natural sites far from load (high transmission cost); uneven/variable output; long costly construction; siltation; seawater corrosion shortens life; low head → many turbines; output varies with lunar cycle.

M210What are the site selection criteria for OTEC? Draw the block diagram and explain the working of an Anderson-cycle (closed) OTEC system.
→ M2-1 (OTEC)

Site criteria: ΔT ≥ 20 °C (warm surface + cold deep water nearby), tropical/subtropical, near land/load, steep shelf for short cold-water pipe, low environmental/socioeconomic cost; chosen by multi-criteria analysis (TOPSIS) on 16-yr SST data + LCOE. Anderson (closed) diagram: warm surface water → evaporator (boils ammonia) → turbine → generator → condenser (cold deep water) → pump → back to evaporator. Working: warm water evaporates low-boiling ammonia; vapor drives turbine/generator; cold deep seawater condenses it; liquid ammonia is pumped around the loop. Working fluid never mixes with seawater.

M25Explain how biofouling affects efficiency of energy conversion and how it can be minimized.
→ M2-1 (Biofouling)

Biofouling = microorganism growth inside evaporator/condenser pipes. It (i) reduces effective heat-transfer area and (ii) lowers performance/efficiency. More surface = more attachment area, scaling with plant size. Minimise: mechanical cleaning (continuous close-fitting balls); chemical cleaning (chlorination/biocides) — release kept to ~1/10 of the environmental limit to limit pollution.

M210Explain the principle of operation of a tidal power plant. How is it classified?
→ M2-2 (Tidal)

Principle: the tidal range (5–15 m) is potential energy. A barrage/dam traps water in a basin at high tide; at low tide the stored water flows through turbines to the sea, driving generators. Modes: ebb, flood, two-way, pumping+turbining. Classification: by basin — (i) Single basin (single ebb / single tide / double cycle, using reversible turbines); (ii) Double basin (upper basin kept higher than lower; turbines between them → smoother output).

M210Draw the layout of a double basin tidal power plant and label all the components. Explain the function of each component.
→ M2-2 (Tidal)

Layout: Sea — dam — Upper basin (high level) — dam (with turbines) — Lower basin (low level) — to sea; sluices in dams. Components: (i) Dam/dyke — barrier creating/between basins, holds head; (ii) Sluiceways — gated channels to fill/drain basins; (iii) Powerhouse — turbines + generators; water flows upper→lower basin through turbines, generating (the lower basin discharges to sea at low tide, upper fills at high tide). Turbines between basins give more continuous output than a single basin.

M25Explain the principle of operation of Ocean Thermal Energy Conversion (OTEC).
→ M2-1 (OTEC)

Surface seawater (~27 °C) is the heat source, deep seawater (~4–7 °C) the heat sink. A heat engine works between them; a minimum ΔT of 20 °C is needed. By Lampert's law radiation falls as I = I₀·e^(−µy), so warm/cold layers stay separate (no convection). Carnot limit η = (T₁−T₂)/T₁ ≈ 6.7% for 27/7 °C — low, but the source is vast and steady.

Module 3–5 — Sample Questions (Syllabus / PYQ)

8 questions
M310Derive the expression for power in a wind turbine. State the Betz limit.
→ M3-1 (Wind)

Mass flow rate m = ρ·A·Vₐ. Power extracted = change in KE/s = ½·m·(V₁²−V₂²) = ½·ρ·A·Vₐ·(V₁²−V₂²), with Vₐ=(V₁+V₂)/2. Differentiating w.r.t. V₂ and setting to zero gives the maximum extractable fraction = 0.5925 (Betz limit) — no turbine exceeds ~59.3% of wind kinetic power. Real Cₚ ≈ 0.35–0.45.

M310Explain the parts, their function and working of a wind power plant. What are the site selection criteria?
→ M3-1 (Wind)

Parts: tower (support + height for wind), nacelle (houses generator, gearbox, controller, yaw drive), rotor blades (wind→mechanical), low-speed shaft, gearbox (speed multiplier, e.g. 1:80), induction generator (mech→elec), pitch drive (blade angle), yaw drive (face wind). Site: avg wind 6–30 m/s year-round; away from cities/forests; measure at several heights; tower survives max local wind speed.

M34What is pitch control of a wind turbine? Explain.
→ M3-1 (Wind)

Pitch control rotates each blade about its axis (via pitch-drive motors) to change the angle of attack as wind speed changes, capturing maximum energy at low wind and spilling excess/protecting the rotor at high wind. It limits power and reduces storm damage.

M410Explain biomass gasification and the types of gasifiers. What are the factors affecting biogas generation?
→ M4-1 (Biomass)

Gasification: thermo-chemical partial combustion (restricted O₂) of biomass → combustible syngas (CO+H₂). Fixed-bed gasifiers: updraft (fuel & air counter-current) and downdraft (co-current; drying→pyrolysis→gas). Biogas factors: feedstock C:N ratio, temperature (~30–35 °C optimum), pH (~neutral), retention time, strict anaerobiosis, mixing/loading rate.

M45Describe the KVIC and Janata biogas plant models.
→ M4-1 (Biomass)

KVIC (floating-drum, constant pressure): underground masonry digester; steel dome floats on slurry, rising/falling with gas; inlet (slurry) + outlet (sludge) pipes; partition wall aids circulation. Janata (fixed-dome, constant volume): fixed dome collects gas, slurry level varies; cheaper, fewer moving parts.

M57Draw the layout of a mini hydro project and explain its working.
→ M5-1 (Hydro)

Layout: intake/weir → desilting chamber → headrace → forebay → penstock → turbine (Pelton/Francis/Kaplan) → generator → transmission; tailrace returns water. Potential energy of stored water drives the turbine; reaction/impulse turbine chosen by head & flow. Maintain ecological flow.

M57Explain the operation of a phosphoric acid fuel cell (PAFC) with a diagram. Describe a PEM fuel cell.
→ M5-1 (Fuel Cells)

PAFC: H₂ → anode splits to H⁺ + e⁻; H⁺ crosses liquid phosphoric-acid electrolyte to cathode; e⁻ power the load; O₂ + H⁺ + e⁻ → H₂O at ~150–200 °C. PEM: solid polymer membrane conducts H⁺; low-temp (~50–100 °C), fast start, used in vehicles/portables; water is the only product.

M57What are the different methods used for the production and storage of hydrogen?
→ M5-1 (Hydrogen)

Production: electrolysis of water (renewable-powered = green H₂); thermo-chemical (steam methane reforming CH₄+H₂O→CO+3H₂, thermolysis, sulphur-iodine cycles); biomass gasification. Storage: compressed gas, cryogenic liquid H₂, metal hydrides, chemical carriers. H₂ is an energy carrier, not a primary source.
